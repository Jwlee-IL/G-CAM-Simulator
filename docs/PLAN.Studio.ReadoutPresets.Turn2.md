### LUT and adjoint tests

- `FloodTopologyTests`: deterministic Gaussian rasters (no sampling variance) on a curved grid with compressed edges and
  unequal heights (corner spot at 8 %), with and without a continuum maximum taller than the weakest crystal. Every
  marker must be a true local maximum of the smoothed density, and every crystal centre must map to its own label (exact).
  A missing corner spot, a missing interior spot or a merged pair must refuse, with no markers and `Lookup = −1`.
- `ChargeAdjointTests`: DPC and corner grid, 4 × 5 and 12 × 12, ideal and 50 Ω inputs. The adjoint solve is compared
  with the legacy solve and with an independent Gauss–Jordan inverse (partial pivoting) of the circuit's conductance
  matrix. The bound comes from conditioning, not from an observed deviation: k = 2 γ(3n) κ∞(G) (asserted < 0.5), then
  `2 [(‖b‖₁‖v‖∞ + max|W|) k/(1−k) + γ(n) ‖b‖₁ (‖v‖∞ + ‖G⁻¹‖∞)]`, and charge imbalance ≤ 4 × bound. The independent
  SPICE-netlist solver agreed on the exported adjoint networks (DPC / corner, 0 / 50 Ω) to 1.2e-15 … 6.5e-15
  (`%TEMP%\gcam-todo41\turn2\adjoint-netlists`; an ad-hoc check, not a committed test). Cancellation during the
  44 × 44 factorisation is observed.
- `ReadoutArtifactTests`: an identical key returns the same artifact. A changed seed or a changed optics value returns a
  new artifact, and the old table is unchanged. A changed network policy is refused on a prepared device. Mutating the
  circuit getters cannot change the netlist. A cancelled token refuses even on a cache hit.

## Pilot (Sharp, one full-budget development seed 41000001)

Started at 16:24 and finished 16:37 with exit 0. Run under `%TEMP%\gcam-todo41\turn2\sharp-pilot-v1\runs\t41-dev-Sharp-v1\41000001\`;
its log file `sharp-pilot-v1.log` is empty, but `done.json` / `stdout.txt` are complete.

- **Cost: 776 s per seed for Sharp; peak working set 3.12 GB.** Stages: build (optics + adjoint network) 35 s; per line,
  calibration 15.5 s, held-out 22–23 s, five rate cells 0.7 s and calibration refinement 66–74 s; photon refinement
  (400 k) 137 s; DOI refinement (16 bins at 400 k) 267 s.
- **Result: the topology-aware LUT v1 failed on all three lines**. At 122 keV the failure was "unsupported lattice slot
  (1, 6)", at 662 keV "unsupported lattice slot (0, 1)", and at 1332 keV the markers were not a regular grid (row 0,
  columns 5/6). Every row was kept, with all denominators.
- **Diagnosis** (substitute; diagnostic probe in `%TEMP%\gcam-todo41\turn2s\diag`, which reproduces the three failures
  exactly):
  1. *Slot geometry.* In noise-free expected raw positions (mid depth, the engine's own optics and network), the DPC
     network bows the edge columns in x by **0.13 / 0.99 / 1.30 / 2.25 local spacings** for Baseline / Sharp / Wide /
     High. The rows stay straight (≤ 0.003 spacing). v1 places axis-aligned slots of ±0.5 spacing around the marginal
     peaks. Those slots cannot contain a column that bows by a spacing or more, and the bowed columns also smear the x
     marginal (at 662 keV the first marginal x peaks fall at bins 117, 119, 144 … against expected column centres at
     54, 83, 112 …). So v1 cannot succeed on any fine geometry, for any seed. The synthetic fixture's curvature is
     ≈ 5 % of a spacing, far milder than the real network.
  2. *Noise-level histogram.* With 32 bins per crystal and σ = 1 bin, the Sharp 662 keV flood has ≈ 1 windowed count
     per bin (952 710 events in 921 600 bins). The smoothed density then has 24 k – 35 k local maxima, about 30 per
     crystal. A "valley one quantum below both maxima" exists between almost any two of them, so on real floods the
     valley test cannot detect a merged pair. The fixtures are noise-free rasters and do not exercise this.
  3. *The true spots are measurable.* For every crystal there is a distinct local maximum within ≤ 5.7 / 3.7 / 2.7 bins
     (≤ 0.2 spacing) of its expected centre at 122 / 662 / 1332 keV, so a topology-aware method can work in principle.
     A naive lattice-growth prototype still ran away into noise maxima, even with 2–3 bins of extra smoothing. The fix
     is development work, not a one-line change.
- **Numerical refinement (photons, DOI)**: maximum angular change 2.27e-5 rad (photons, 100 k → 400 k) and 1.59e-5 rad
  (DOI, 8 → 16 bins), against Sharp's allocation (pitch / D) / √12 = 2.165e-3 rad. That is 1.05 % and 0.73 % of the
  allocation (single seed). The calibration component could not be measured, because the LUT failed.

## Freeze gate and residual numerical error

The primary implementer's statement — "small refinement differences alone do not bound the reference recipe's remaining
numerical error" — means the following. Development compares the recipe level (N) with one refinement (4N photons,
2× DOI bins, 4× histories with 2× bins). A small difference |θ_N − θ_4N| shows that the result moved little between
the two levels. It does not show how far either level is from the converged value. That needs a convergence model or
a third level: a slowly converging quantity can change little per step and still carry a large residual. Rule v1
therefore hard-codes `ResidualErrorEstablished: false`, and the selector refuses a freeze whatever the data show.

Proposal for rule v2, to bound PD-6's "numerical error ≤ 10 % of the spatial allocation":
1. **Stochastic components are already in the statistic.** Every outer seed draws its own optical table (`Key(seed, 1)`),
   calibration flood and response streams. Their Monte Carlo noise is therefore part of the between-seed variance that
   the one-sided 95 % bound already covers. Only the *bias* of the finite recipe (DOI bins, histogram bin size and
   smoothing, finite-photon bias of the ratio position estimator, finite-history bias of the LUT) needs a separate
   bound.
2. **Bound each bias by three-level Richardson, with an observed order.** For each component, measure θ at levels L, 2L
   (or 4L) and 4L (or 16L) on the DEV4 seeds. Let r be the contraction ratio of successive differences (upper 95 %
   seed-cluster bound of Δ₂ / Δ₁). Accept the component only if r ≤ r_max (0.5 is proposed; MC-type terms contract
   by 0.5 per 4× budget, first-order discretisation by 0.5 per 2× bins). Its residual at level L is then
   ≤ Δ₁ / (1 − r) ≤ 2 Δ₁. The freeze condition is Σ over components of upper95(Δ₁) / (1 − r) ≤ 0.1 × allocation per
   axis. If any component fails the contraction check, it is refused, never extrapolated.
3. **Cost.** The third level is the expensive part. For optics a 16× photon level is impractical for High, so use
   DOI 8 / 16 / 32 bins at fixed photons and photons 100 k / 400 k plus the seed-variance argument of item 1. The pilot
   already shows a large margin there: 2 × (1.05 % + 0.73 %) ≈ 3.6 % of the allocation. For calibration, the levels
   are 2 000 / 8 000 / 32 000 histories per crystal at constant raw smoothing width.
4. Rule v2 and recipe v2 get new pins and a new manifest. DEV4 seeds stay development seeds (method iteration on them
   is legitimate); selection and confirmation stay untouched.

## Findings that need a planner / author decision before any family runs

1. **LUT method v2 (development).** Columns of the DPC flood bow by 1–2.3 spacings, and the 32-bin / σ = 1 histogram
   is at shot-noise level. Directions, all from measured data and none inserting truth:
   - a lattice-following matcher, either row bands from the straight rows followed by per-row x maxima, or growth that
     extrapolates from two assigned neighbours with a bounded vector change;
   - peak detection that is robust to noise: smoothing matched to the measured spot scale, or a Poisson significance
     test for the valley instead of "one quantum";
   - realistic fixtures: the engine's noise-free expected positions as the lattice shape (bow up to 2.3 spacings),
     sampled with Poisson spots at the recipe's density (≈ 1 count per bin).

   The histogram scale and smoothing are part of the numerical recipe that development freezes (PD-6), so changing
   them is in scope.
2. **The acceptance gate is noise-limited in the rate cells.** A rate cell has 10 000 hits, i.e. ≈ 11 scored per crystal
   per seed for Sharp and ≈ 5 for High. Over 32 confirmation seeds at 662 keV and 1e3 cps (windowed 35 %), Sharp has
   ≈ 125 assigned per crystal, a binomial relative s.d. ≈ 7 %, and an expected minimum of 900 crystals ≈ 0.77 × the
   median. High comes out ≈ 0.64; Sharp at 1332 keV and 1e6 cps comes out ≈ 0.4. A perfect, uniform readout would
   therefore fail "weakest ≥ 0.8 × median" from counting noise alone. Options (author / planner):
   - (a) scale the rate-cell budget per crystal (≈ 3 000 per crystal; rate processing costs ≈ 13 µs per hit, so
     ≈ +9 min per seed for Sharp and ≈ +20 min for High);
   - (b) gate on a Bonferroni lower confidence bound of the weakest crystal instead of the point value;
   - (c) gate per-crystal acceptance on the isolated held-out rows (3 000 per crystal; relative s.d. < 1 % over 32 seeds)
     and treat the rate-dependent loss as global.
3. **Selection can be skipped if development leaves one frozen candidate.** Selection exists to choose among candidates
   on seeds that are independent of confirmation, so that the winner's selection bias never reaches the confirmation
   statistic. With one frozen method and recipe there is no choice and so no selection bias, and CONFIRM32 stays a
   fresh, disjoint test (rule v1 already allows a single frozen candidate to bypass selection). Any iteration on DEV4
   is itself a selection, but it happens on seeds disjoint from confirmation, so confirmation is still unbiased. The
   one practical reason to keep SELECTION16 is to protect CONFIRM32: a failed confirmation spends the single REPAIR32
   family. Recommendation: skip selection when DEV4 shows every calibration succeeding with clear margin on every
   geometry and line; otherwise run SELECTION16 as a dry check.
