**At a glance**
- No source treats Cs-137 under a Co-60 continuum as unresolvable; every one treats it as a background to subtract,
  paid for in counting statistics (Currie: L_D ≈ 2.71 + 4.65√B).
- The one documented Cs / Co image subtraction (photopeak gate minus a same-width gate above it) is a **pinhole**
  camera (GUALI, source 1); no coded-aperture example was found. The subtraction is linear, so it carries over.
- No published Co : Cs ratio limit for scintillator-camera imaging was found — Gcam's ≈ 2 : 1 spatial limit and its
  stripping results are simulation results for this geometry, not checked against a literature value.
- **Carry into the TODO-35 plan:** (1) the Cs-137 detection limit versus Co : Cs and live time (expected
  MDA_Cs ∝ √(A_Co / t)); (2) the **systematic error of R** — at high Co : Cs a few-percent error leaves a residual
  coded from Co-60's direction, i.e. a false "Cs" hot spot at Co-60's position; no source quantifies it;
  (3) the coded-aperture multiplexing penalty (Fenimore) on top of the spectral cost; (4) an MLEM with each isotope's
  full crystal response as the principled successor of per-pixel stripping.


## 1. Methods used in gamma imagers

**Side-window ("Compton suppression gate") image subtraction.** This is the method best documented for exactly the Cs/Co case, but in a *pinhole* scintillator camera, not a coded-aperture one: GUALI (IFIC/ENRESA; PSPMT Anger readout, 7.6 % FWHM at 662 keV). [S] Caballero et al. 2018 (JINST 13 P03016), §3.3:
> "the Compton spectra generated by higher energy photopeaks from other radioisotopes contributions inside of the selected gate will degenerate the intended photopeak image. In order to subtract this background contribution, a similar gate in width is placed just on the right side of the photopeak … After reconstructing both images, from the photopeak and the Compton suppression gates, we subtract the latter from the former, so that the resulting clean image contains mostly the desired radioisotope photopeak contribution. … In the 137Cs case, the 662 keV photopeak gate has a contribution coming from the 60Co Compton continuum."

[S] The same paper says that 7.6 % "is enough since it allows one to distinguish the two most common radioisotopes expected in NPP scenarios (137Cs and 60Co)". It gives no error budget for the subtraction.

[I] The side-gate method estimates the same thing as our per-pixel R × (Co photopeak) stripping, from a different reference window. Because the subtraction is linear, it carries over to coded-aperture decoding: subtracting the images after decoding gives the same result as subtracting the gates per pixel before decoding.

**Coded-aperture commercial and research systems.**
- [S] GAMPIX (CEA LIST) is a Timepix + 1 mm CdTe camera with a coded mask, sold by Mirion/Canberra as iPIX. The EDF/CEA poster (IRPA) lists only two background modes: "1 shot + average background subtraction" and "2 shots … mask … anti-mask". It names spectrum assessment as future work.
- [S] The iPIX spec sheet describes a "3-Band energy discriminator" that can "differentiate energies related to multiple hot spots". Nuclide identification comes from a separate 1 cm³ CZT add-on (iPIX-NID, < 2.5 % FWHM at 662 keV), not from the imaging head.
- [S] The ANIMMA 2013 GAMPIX imaging-spectrometer work (HAL in2p3-01024125) compares cluster-size and ToT energy estimation and finds ToT better. Amoyal et al. 2019 (NIM A 944, 162568) characterise GAMPIX metrologically; full text not read.
- [S] The Caliste-based CEA camera (Maier et al., arXiv:2001.10224) claims "localization and spectro-identification of radionuclides" (abstract only).
- No source found documents how any coded-aperture product handles a lower line sitting under a higher line's continuum.
- [S] CARTOGAM (Gal et al. 2001) is the earlier CEA pinhole camera.
- [S] Korean coded-aperture work: Im et al. 2017/2018 (IEEE NSS, SiPM, 2×2 mosaic of 19×19 MURA, 14.5 % energy resolution) report imaging only. The abstract has no multi-isotope separation.
- Not verified (No checkable technical documentation, so they are left out): RadCam, N-Visage, Kromek "Gamma Imager-H".

**Joint spectral-spatial reconstruction.**
- [S] Energy-Imaging Integrated Deconvolution (EIID) is MLEM over a combined (direction × energy) source space (Xu & He 2007, NIM A 574, 98). Wang, Jaworski, Kaye & He (IEEE NSS 2009, p. 1065) describe it as able to deconvolve "the source image at any specific energy, as well as the incident spectrum from any direction". By including Compton-continuum events in the system model it recovers peak branching ratios. This is on 3-D CZT (Compton), not a coded aperture.
- [S] Hamel/Marleau et al. (INMM 2015) use MLEM with an MC-computed response to unfold a spectrum per pixel, and this "isolates the energy spectra for each individual source in a multi-source field" (Cf-252 + Co-60, abstract).
- [I] The coded-aperture analogue is an MLEM whose system matrix includes the full energy response (photopeak + crystal Compton) for each isotope. That is the principled generalisation of per-pixel stripping.

**Full-spectrum / ML decomposition.**
- [S] NMF of gamma spectra with source templates (Bilton et al. 2019, IEEE TNS 66, 827) does detection and identification for non-imaging spectra.
- [S] List-mode multi-energy-window reconstruction exists in SPECT (Rahman et al., arXiv:2305.17111).
- No coded-aperture paper that applies NMF or ML unmixing per pixel to the Cs/Co problem.

## 2. Statistical cost

- [S] Currie 1968 (Anal. Chem. 40, 586) defines the critical level L_C and detection limit L_D from the blank variance. In the well-known paired-blank form, L_C ≈ 2.33√B and L_D ≈ 2.71 + 4.65√B for B background counts in the peak window (also given in ISO 11929 and Gilmore's textbook).
- [S] Ahn et al. (Appl. Radiat. Isot. 69 (2011) 1294; arXiv:0901.1359), on Cs in soil with HPGe: "The minimum detectable activity depends on the square root of the number of background events which mainly come from three different reasons: The sample itself, Compton continuum, and natural radioactivity". Removing the K-40 continuum source chemically "reduced the MDA by almost one order of magnitude".
- [I] Applied to our case:
  - The net Cs count is N_Cs = G − R·P_Co, with variance G + R²·P_Co (plus the variance of R itself, if R is calibrated).
  - With the Co continuum dominating, B ∝ A_Co·t, so MDA_Cs ∝ √(A_Co/t). The Cs that can be detected therefore grows as the square root of the Co activity. Nothing makes it impossible; it only costs time. Halving the window width (better resolution) cuts B by about 2× and the MDA by about √2.
  - GAGG at 6–7 % is close to NaI (~7 %), so it gains little over NaI. Against LaBr3 (~3 %), whose window is about 2× narrower, GAGG has about √2 worse MDA, assuming a continuum that is flat across the window.
- [S] Fenimore 1978 (Appl. Opt. 17, 3562): the coded-aperture signal-to-noise ratio is set by *all* counts on the detector, not only those from the point source. [I] So the continuum counts add noise to every decoded pixel, not just to Co-60's direction. This multiplexing penalty adds to the spectral √B cost. It is why spatial separation alone fails at moderate Co:Cs.
- **No published MDA numbers were found specifically for Cs-137 in Co-60 mixtures** with NaI, CsI, GAGG or LaBr3 imaging resolution.
- [S] The reverse case is published: Kaburagi et al. 2022 (J. Nucl. Sci. Technol. 59, 983; CeBr3). Co-60 was identified under an intense Cs-137 field after subtracting the Cs spectrum, up to ~0.95 Sv/h (abstract or review summary only).

## 3. "Cannot be resolved" or "resolvable but costly"?

- [S] Every source found treats it as a **background to subtract**. None treats it as a fundamental limit:
  - GUALI calls it a contribution that will "degenerate the intended photopeak image" and subtracts it to get a "clean image".
  - Ahn et al. treat the Compton continuum as one of the background terms entering the MDA through √B.
  - EIID and the Hamel et al. MLEM work model the continuum explicitly in order to unfold it.
- **No** statement was found that Cs under a Co-60 continuum is unresolvable in principle.
- [I] The real limits are statistical (√B), plus systematic errors in R: R depends on the gain, the window, the source angle and the scatter environment. As the Co:Cs ratio grows, a few-percent error in R produces a residual that is comparable to the Cs signal. That residual is coded from the Co-60 direction, so it appears as a false "Cs" hot spot at Co-60's position. No source found quantifies this.

## 4. Published Co:Cs activity-ratio limit for scintillator-camera imaging of Cs

- **None found.**
- GUALI shows Cs and Co images in the field (container: 21.9 vs 7.3 Bq/g, a Cs:Co ratio of about 3:1, so Cs dominated). It quotes no limiting ratio.
- [I] Our Co:Cs ≈ 2:1 limit for spatial-only separation, and our stripping results, have no published counterpart that was found; they are presented as simulation results specific to this geometry, not checked against a literature value.
